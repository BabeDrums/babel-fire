# Babel FIRE — Core Product Doctrine

**Status:** canonical cross-generation FIRE product doctrine. Generation specs describe implementation behavior; they do not override this file unless doctrine is deliberately changed in the same work.

## Naming

- **V6** — current production implementation generation.
- **G7** — frozen successor shell generation. Technical identifiers remain `V7_DRAFT.md`, `/v7-preview/`, and `production-projects-v1-v7-preview` unless a real migration reason appears.
- **FIRE Core** — first complete functional product: idea or reference → finished, release-ready audio with FIRE acting as a useful production companion.
- **FIRE 1.0** — future validated release milestone after FIRE Core proves itself on real projects and is intentionally promoted.

## Product promise

FIRE helps a musician answer:

> **What deserves my attention next, why, and what should I try in the DAW?**

FIRE is a production companion / decision engine, not a DAW replacement, plugin encyclopedia, giant questionnaire, automatic song generator, generic checklist, or mandatory course.

Success criterion:

> **After a useful FIRE session, the musician should know what to do in the DAW that they did not clearly know before opening FIRE. If they only updated FIRE, FIRE failed.**

FIRE Core ends at a finished, release-ready audio file plus essential delivery checks/basic metadata. Distribution, artwork, marketing, and scheduling remain outside the current Core boundary.

FIRE must remain useful as a standalone specialist. A musician should be able to open FIRE, make or finish a record, and leave satisfied without first creating a World profile, defining a career plan, or entering the wider Babel ecosystem.

## One FIRE brain, two first-class project types

### Original
Start with musical material, an idea, a demo, or an already-developed production and move it toward a finished record. The target develops from the musician's intention, the material that exists, and accepted decisions made while the record develops. Guidance should organize decisions without policing creativity.

An Original may enter FIRE with almost nothing or with a nearly finished record. FIRE should orient to the current state instead of forcing a restart.

### Recreation / Cover
Use an existing song to make a faithful cover, reconstruction, interpretation, reimagining, or production study. Recreation is a complete standalone workflow, not a secondary learning mode and not dependent on Original.

One Recreation engine can carry a mutable creative bias:
- **Match** — approach the reference as faithfully as practical
- **Interpret** — preserve identity while making meaningful choices your own
- **Reimagine** — freely change arrangement, instrumentation, feel, pacing, sound, or other production dimensions

The bias need not be a mandatory first question if the relationship is already known or inferable.

For an individual difference, FIRE may classify it as:
- **Match** — reduce the difference
- **Intentional** — the difference belongs to this version
- **Explore** — unresolved; keep it available for experimentation

Original and Recreation use the same production engine. Their main difference is the source of the target:
- Recreation: reference + musician relationship to it
- Original: musician intention + existing material + evolving accepted decisions

## Core operating model

The product cycle is:

**Target → Production Map → Active Focus → FIRE Loop → DAW → Evaluate → Update → Next Focus**

Repeat until there is no currently justified production work valuable enough to block delivery.

The layers have different jobs:
- **Target** — what the record is being judged against.
- **Production Map** — where the record currently stands across production domains.
- **Active Focus** — what deserves attention now.
- **FIRE Loop** — how FIRE reasons through one meaningful issue/opportunity.
- **DAW** — where the musician actually makes the record.
- **Evaluation** — new evidence that updates the Map and next focus.

The decision engine is therefore not the entire product and the roadmap/map is not a rigid course. The Map provides orientation; Active Focus navigates it.

## Production Map

The current Core production domains are:

1. **Material & Form** — melody, lyrics, harmony, rhythm/groove, tempo/pulse, sections, form, motifs; enough musical material to support production decisions.
2. **Arrangement & Roles** — what appears when, density, contrast, instrumentation/elements, focal hierarchy, and the jobs major elements perform.
3. **Source & Performance** — what creates the sounds and how the sources/performances should behave: articulation, feel, energy, character, playing/singing approach.
4. **Capture & Construction** — recording, programming, sampling, synthesis, layering, and otherwise creating usable production material.
5. **Edit & Assembly** — comping, timing, tuning, chopping, cleanup, alignment, take/part selection, and assembly.
6. **Balance & Hierarchy** — levels, pan, foreground/background relationships, masking relationships, low-end relationships, and focal order.
7. **Tone & Dynamics** — spectral balance, transient behavior, compression, saturation/distortion, density, and dynamic relationships.
8. **Space & Movement** — depth, width, ambience, reverb/delay, automation, transitions, evolving energy, and motion.
9. **Translation & Finish** — translation outside the session, overall coherence, delivery checks, meaningful remaining blockers, and the stopping judgment.

These are **domains, not compulsory stages** and not necessarily final screen names.

A blank project has a useful default dependency direction, but FIRE must not treat the Map as a locked staircase. Later evidence can reopen earlier work when the real cause is upstream.

### Domain state

A domain is not simply complete/incomplete. Current Core reasoning distinguishes roughly:
- not yet relevant / not assessed
- active
- **provisionally stable under current evidence**
- reopened because new evidence invalidated an earlier assumption

The key rule is:

> **Prevent what can reasonably be prevented now. Commit when there is enough evidence. Reopen only when new evidence earns it.**

FIRE should avoid both failure modes:
- **premature downstream work** — moving on despite an already-visible upstream problem
- **endless upstream perfection** — refusing to create new evidence until every possible uncertainty is solved theoretically

The correct time to move forward is when the remaining uncertainty is better resolved by making/hearing more of the real record.

### Domain decision contract

Each domain is evaluated internally through four questions:
1. what should FIRE help prevent here before downstream investment?
2. what evidence is genuinely required?
3. when is this domain stable enough under current evidence?
4. what later evidence would legitimately reopen it?

These are reasoning rules, not a checklist the musician must fill out.

Current stable-enough/reopen expectations:

- **Material & Form** — stable when enough coherent material/form exists to make the next production decision without obvious waste; reopen when later production reveals the song material itself is the blocker.
- **Arrangement & Roles** — stable when major roles, hierarchy, density and section contrast are coherent enough to justify building/recording; reopen when realized sources/performances create a role or density conflict that was not reasonably knowable earlier.
- **Source & Performance** — stable when important sources/performances plausibly serve their roles without a known problem that makes processing premature; reopen when context reveals the source/performance behaves differently than expected.
- **Capture & Construction** — stable when necessary material exists and is technically/creatively usable enough that downstream work has higher value than recapture/rebuild; reopen when an apparent later problem is actually baked into capture/construction.
- **Edit & Assembly** — stable when no known edit/assembly issue materially distracts from the intended performance or blocks useful balance/tone decisions; reopen when later processing/context exposes an edit that previously seemed acceptable.
- **Balance & Hierarchy** — stable when the record communicates a believable hierarchy before deeper processing; reopen when tone/space/automation or new material changes the perceived hierarchy.
- **Tone & Dynamics** — stable when important elements behave tonally/dynamically in ways that support their jobs and no known issue meaningfully blocks the record; reopen when later changes alter those relationships.
- **Space & Movement** — stable when depth/width/ambience/motion reinforce intention and hierarchy and more complexity is not currently providing meaningful improvement; reopen when translation, balance or arrangement exposes a spatial/movement issue.
- **Translation & Finish** — finished when no known issue meaningfully prevents the record from sounding intentional, convincing and release-ready for its target; reopen only when a real translation/delivery check exposes a meaningful previously unknown issue.

Reopening is therefore not an excuse for weak earlier guidance. If FIRE could reasonably identify the problem in the owning domain before downstream work, it should do so.

## Active Focus

The Production Map answers:

> **Where does the record stand?**

Active Focus answers:

> **What deserves attention right now?**

FIRE should choose the current focus using evidence such as:
- upstream leverage
- consequence
- target relevance
- unresolved uncertainty
- downstream dependency/blocking
- cost and reversibility of a useful experiment
- current creative momentum
- musician constraints
- known project history
- whether one upstream decision could solve several downstream symptoms

Only one meaningful focus should normally dominate a focused FIRE session.

If evidence supports one strongest current move, FIRE should recommend it. If multiple filtered actions are genuinely valid, use the existing guidance language:
- **Recommended** — strongest current move
- **Alternative** — another valid solution with a different tradeoff
- **Explore** — unconventional/identity-seeking direction

A playful random selector such as a future **Follow the Spark** belongs only after FIRE has already filtered the space to genuinely viable choices. It must never randomize perception, intention, preference, or factual project state.

## Shared reasoning engine

The FIRE loop is:

1. **Feel** — what do you perceive and what matters?
2. **Identify** — where is the meaningful difference/problem/opportunity?
3. **Reason** — what likely causes it and what kind of decision is this?
4. **Experiment** — make one intentional change/test in the DAW.
5. **Evaluate** — did it move toward the target, teach something useful, or reveal another problem?

The loop operates on the current Active Focus; it is not the entire record lifecycle.

Meaningful issues can finish as:
- **Resolved**
- **Accepted**
- **Unresolved**

This helps prevent perfection loops. Recreation does not require an indistinguishable clone unless faithful matching is actually the goal.

Every recommendation to add, remove, process, automate, double, widen, or otherwise change something should answer:

> **What job would this change perform, and why might it help this specific production?**

If FIRE cannot explain that, it should not recommend the change.

## Recreation reference overlay

Recreation has a persistent reference layer across the Production Map rather than a single late "final comparison" stage.

The reference may inform:
- form and tempo/pulse
- harmony/melody/groove
- arrangement and instrumentation
- source/performance character
- balance and hierarchy
- tone/dynamics
- space/movement
- translation/finish

Reference comparison is therefore available throughout the work. A meaningful difference may be Match, Intentional, or Explore at any domain.

A blank Recreation may often move roughly:

**understand reference → map material/form → arrangement/roles → source/performance → capture/construction → edit/assembly → balance/hierarchy → tone/dynamics → space/movement → translation/finish**

That is a useful default traversal, not a universal compulsory recipe.

## Original target development

Original uses the same Production Map without a finished external master defining the target.

Early in an Original, **Target ↔ Material** may develop together. FIRE should not require a perfect creative brief before making music. A sentence of intention, a groove, a sound, a lyric, a melody, an existing demo, or other meaningful material may be enough to start.

As the record develops, accepted decisions make the target more concrete. If a developed Original enters FIRE, earlier domains may already be provisionally stable and FIRE should begin wherever current leverage exists.

FIRE is not a general-purpose songwriting generator. It may help get enough musical material moving for production to continue, but the artist remains the author.

## FIRE Knowledge model

FIRE combines three knowledge/evidence layers:

### Shared production knowledge
Research-backed production principles, dependencies, tradeoffs, common failure modes, diagnostics, and useful experiments. This layer may be large and information-rich without becoming user data entry.

### Project knowledge
Small, cumulative meaning specific to the record: target, structure/roles where relevant, intentions, observations, accepted decisions, experiments/results, provisional domain state, unresolved problems, and continuation point.

### Current evidence
What the record/work is telling FIRE now: a heard problem, a result from an experiment, a reference difference, a new constraint, or another observation that may change the next decision.

The core design principle is:

> **Deep model underneath. Minimal burden on the musician.**

For every meaningful Core decision, FIRE should ask internally:
- what production knowledge is relevant?
- what project information already exists?
- what can be inferred from prior work or a Recreation reference?
- what evidence is actually missing?
- would an experiment reveal the answer better than a question?
- what musician preference/skill evidence materially changes the guidance?
- what recommendation can FIRE explain confidently?
- what should be preserved afterward?

Adapt by domain, not one global Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced label. Do not front-load a large musician questionnaire. Learn progressively through real work and timely clarification.

## Question rule

There is no giant predetermined question tree.

Before FIRE asks the musician something, it checks:
1. do I already know it?
2. did the musician answer it earlier?
3. can project state tell me?
4. can a Recreation reference tell me?
5. can valid incoming Babel context tell me?
6. would a small DAW experiment reveal it better than asking?
7. would the answer materially change the next useful recommendation or prevent a bad one?

If not, do not ask.

Questions about perception, preference, intention, and factual state are **reporting nodes** and must not be randomized. `I don't know` / `Not sure` is valid and may open a small discriminating experiment or explanation instead of blocking progress.

Choice/randomization is allowed only after FIRE has narrowed the space to genuinely viable action alternatives.

## Learning is ambient, optional, and applied

Making the record is always the primary task.

- teach through real production decisions whenever possible
- default to concise guidance; reveal deeper **Why?** progressively
- instructional detours must be skippable
- a quick-cover user must be able to move with minimal explanation
- FIRE may quietly retain skill evidence, lessons, repeated weaknesses, successful approaches, and explicit preferences
- page completion is not mastery

A future optional Practice / Skills / Growth surface may summarize development, but learning progress must not become mandatory workflow chrome. A separate learning product is not earned merely because FIRE teaches; wider product questions belong to Babel Truth/Lab, not FIRE doctrine.

## Companion boundary

Do not recreate the DAW inside FIRE.

FIRE should mainly retain meaning the DAW usually does not understand well:
- target / intention / hierarchy
- relevant production-map state
- what was heard
- diagnosis
- meaningful experiment + intended job + result
- accepted decision/difference
- resolved / accepted / unresolved issue
- reusable lesson when it genuinely helps
- next continuation / Active Focus

Do not require detailed routing, plugin settings, every take/region, every automation value, or every knob movement unless a future feature proves that information necessary.

Default rhythm:

**FIRE → Logic → FIRE → Logic**

FIRE Core does not require audio-aware analysis or direct Logic/session integration. Without those capabilities, FIRE must be explicit about what evidence it actually has. Future audio/session analysis may reduce user reporting burden if real use earns it.

## Track → Element → Role — hypothesis

Current breadboarding hypothesis:
- **Track** — represented DAW/recorded part
- **Element** — perceptual production object that may contain one or several tracks
- **Role** — job the element performs: focal point, pulse, harmonic support, low-end movement, atmosphere, width, texture, etc.

Purpose: reason at listener-perception level without duplicating Logic's mixer. Do not lock exact data/UI before real projects prove it.

## Fire visualization — hypothesis

Do not define the Fire as percent-complete, XP, or mastery.

The strongest current semantic direction is:

> **Where does this record currently have heat?**

Possible future behavior:
- current Active Focus burns strongest
- provisionally stable areas settle
- unresolved areas retain heat
- new evidence can reignite an earlier domain
- Recreation heat may reflect meaningful unresolved reference differences

The first Core implementation does not require this visualization. Its exact representation remains an active hypothesis until the underlying state model proves useful in real work.

## First FIRE Core implementation proof

The first implementation intentionally does **not** build all nine domains, a complete roadmap UI, a universal question engine, or the final Fire visualization.

It proves one thin vertical behavior:

**open project → FIRE shows one Active Focus → explains why → gives one useful DAW experiment → musician returns with the result → FIRE preserves the result → project/domain state updates → next Active Focus changes**

The evaluation proof supports at least:
- **Helped** — current domain may become provisionally stable and FIRE can move to the next useful focus
- **Didn't help** — domain remains active; FIRE should try a cheaper alternate test before escalating
- **Revealed something else** — new evidence can reopen/redirect the current route instead of blindly advancing

Two real fixtures prove the shared spine:
- a real Recreation/reference case (current proof: `Creep`)
- the Original `Even`, using already-established artist-authored intention instead of re-asking it

Controlled fixture logic is acceptable in this first proof. The goal is to prove the interaction/state contract before building a generalized production-recommendation engine.

Success means the deployed proof lets a musician:

**open song → understand what FIRE thinks deserves attention → understand why → receive one useful Logic experiment → return with result → see FIRE remember it → receive a meaningfully changed next focus**

without walking through `Setup → Structure → Tracks`, entering a large profile, or maintaining FIRE instead of making music.

The existing G7 `Setup → Structure → Tracks` fixture remains disposable shell-certification content until this Core proof earns replacement. Do not promote that old fixture into Core merely because it is implemented.

## Cross-app boundary

Babel Truth ADR 0005 governs cross-app context. FIRE owns detailed production knowledge and should not expose its entire internal reasoning model merely because another Babel product exists.

If a FIRE fact genuinely matters outside FIRE, expose only the smallest meaningful projection needed by the destination. Candidate examples to prove through real use include:
- stable song/record identity and type
- explicit intent relevant outside production
- relationship to a larger body of work
- high-level production state or continuation point
- meaningful accepted decisions another product should not re-ask
- provenance indicating FIRE owns the production source state
- a destination/return context for continuing production

Do not lock a universal schema from these examples. Prove the fields through real FIRE → World → FIRE or future specialist flows first.

FIRE should not advertise World mechanically after every project. The specialist must provide value first. An optional “take this further” invitation is appropriate only when the user's work creates a genuinely new broader possibility, and it should not repeat once that possibility has already been accepted, declined, or established elsewhere.

Likewise, when another Babel product sends a user into FIRE with valid context, FIRE should use that context rather than starting from a blank onboarding question.

## Larger bodies of work — active hypothesis

FIRE currently treats Original and Recreation/Cover as production workflows for a record/song. A larger body of work such as a single, EP, or album is a different dimension and should not be added as a third peer project type beside Original/Cover without evidence.

Breadboarding should test whether FIRE needs a lightweight relationship above individual song projects so multiple FIRE records can belong to one larger music project while FIRE owns only the production facet. Album-level production capabilities such as sequencing, transitions, cohesion, and delivery consistency must be earned through real project use rather than invented as a suite up front.

World or another future product may understand a broader album context, but FIRE must still support larger music work independently if that production job is proven useful.
