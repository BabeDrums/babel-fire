# Babel — App Concept Ledger

**Status:** Future product-concept authority for the wider Babel ecosystem. This file preserves what is actually known about possible sibling apps without pretending that brainstorms are finished products. It does not authorize implementation work and does not override `FIRE_CORE.md` for FIRE.

Use this file with `BABEL_START_HERE.md` and `BABEL_PLATFORM.md` whenever a conversation is about surrounding Babel apps, Babel World, a future learning product, visual identity, elemental names, or cross-app relationships.

## Core rule

> **Product need earns an app. Naming and metaphor follow the job.**

Do not invent apps merely to:

- complete an elemental set
- populate Babel World
- make a product-family diagram look balanced
- give every creative discipline its own icon
- justify shared infrastructure before a second real product exists

A small number of excellent, genuinely distinct products is better than a complete-looking family of unnecessary apps.

Babel prefers short, simple, durable names when one word can carry the idea cleanly. That naming preference must never decide scope before the product job is understood.

## What exists now

### Babel FIRE — active product

FIRE is the production companion / decision engine.

Its job is to help a musician move from musical material or a reference toward finished, release-ready audio while answering:

> **What deserves my attention next, why, and what should I try in the DAW?**

FIRE has two first-class project types:

- **Original** — idea/material → finished record
- **Recreation** — reference → faithful cover, reconstruction, interpretation, reimagining, or production study

Recreation is not merely a learning mode. Someone may use FIRE only to make covers/recreations efficiently and never use Original or any future learning surface.

FIRE already teaches contextually through real production work. A future optional **Practice / Skills / Growth** surface inside FIRE can provide more deliberate training without turning FIRE into a mandatory course.

## Candidate products — not commitments

### Dedicated learning product — not currently earned

Babel has discussed a possible future learning-focused sibling, but **there is no approved learning app yet and no approved name for it**.

The current default is to keep production learning inside FIRE through contextual guidance plus optional Practice / Skills / Growth.

A separate learning product becomes justified only when deliberate training becomes a sufficiently distinct job that keeping it inside FIRE would make FIRE worse. Examples that could eventually justify a split include:

- substantial progressive production curriculum
- ear-training laboratories
- structured production drills
- blind A/B or diagnosis exercises
- project-independent practice paths
- deeper deliberate-practice systems that are useful even when no active song needs them

If such a product eventually exists:

- it should use the same future **Babel Learning Contract** for genuinely shared production skills/evidence
- credible overlapping evidence may strengthen the same production-skill model used by FIRE
- merely opening or progressing through the app must never grow the Fire
- its own local journey/metaphor may be different from FIRE
- it must remain useful on its own rather than existing only to feed FIRE

Do **not** currently broaden this into a universal app for studying every creative discipline. Ideas such as cinematography studies, rollout analysis, photography studies, live-show pacing, visual-design lessons, or unrelated creative curricula may someday become legitimate jobs, but they are **not part of the current learning-product definition**.

The useful distinction is not simply “FIRE creates, learning studies.” FIRE itself already includes Recreation, study, diagnosis, practice, and learning. The split is earned only when **deliberate training itself** becomes a separate enough product job.

### Visual Identity product — promising candidate, deliberately underdefined

Babel has identified **visual identity** as a genuinely promising separate product area worth exploring after FIRE fundamentals are further along.

Working job hypothesis:

> **Help turn the identity of an artist, era, song, or creative project into a coherent visual language.**

Possible concerns may eventually include:

- visual vocabulary
- color
- materials and textures
- symbols and recurring objects
- environments / locations
- typography
- styling / clothing
- lighting
- photography language
- video / cinematography direction
- artwork direction
- stage/world atmosphere
- what visually belongs vs what does not

These are **brainstorming boundaries, not a locked feature list**. Do not design a giant mood-board app merely because these categories exist. The product job, workflow, and evidence of need still have to be discovered.

No final app name, element, metaphor, visual system, or relationship to FIRE is locked.

### Babel World — optional future hub hypothesis

**Babel World** is the strongest current hub concept. It is not being built now and must never become required for FIRE or another sibling app.

Possible future jobs include:

- cross-app identity
- an ecosystem-level home once multiple real products exist
- meaningful shared progress/evidence views where aggregation is legitimate
- source/origin visibility
- achievements, badges, collections, and discoveries
- app handoffs
- account/entitlement continuity
- optional lore/world-building connections

Babel World is **not currently defined as the universal database or source of truth for every song, era, project, image, or creative object**. A future shared object/ID model may be useful, but it must be earned by real cross-app workflows.

Each specialized app should own its task-specific data. Shared infrastructure should own only concepts that genuinely need to transfer.

World must not:

- duplicate every app's working UI
- become a mandatory launcher
- create one global Babel level or skill score
- force every product into the same metaphor
- invent sibling apps simply to give the hub content

#### Collection / spatial-world direction

The final presentation is intentionally unresolved.

Babel has liked possibilities such as:

- gallery
- trophy case
- map
- neighborhood
- park
- path
- traversable world
- another more original structure discovered from the content itself

It may eventually feel like a place the user can move through rather than a static grid. The right shape should emerge from the real badges, apps, progression artifacts, and relationships that eventually exist.

> **The collection earns its shape.**

Do not design an empty world first and then invent products/rewards to populate it.

## Elemental names are not product slots

FIRE's name naturally suggests elemental language, and **WATER has been floated historically as a possible future sibling-app name/example**. That does **not** mean WATER is currently a product, and it does not mean WATER is the learning app or the visual-identity app.

Likewise, **AIR** and **EARTH** are not approved product definitions.

Rules:

- never create apps merely to complete Fire / Water / Air / Earth
- never assign a product job to an element because the name sounds aesthetically satisfying
- if a future product earns existence first and an elemental metaphor later fits it unusually well, the naming can be reconsidered then
- it is acceptable for the ecosystem to have only FIRE plus one or two non-elemental sibling names

## Shared evidence, local identity

The strongest cross-app architecture principle remains:

> **Shared evidence, local identity.**

When two real products genuinely observe the same capability, they should eventually share stable evidence semantics rather than inventing incompatible meanings.

This does **not** mean every product shares one UI, one progression metaphor, one project database, or one global score.

A future **Babel Learning Contract** should eventually define stable, versioned meanings for overlapping concepts such as:

- skill/domain identity
- evidence type
- source app/context
- project/practice context
- guidance depth
- demonstrated transfer
- confidence/strength interpretation inputs
- schema/version provenance

The architecture lesson remains:

> **Evidence first, interpretation second.**

Store what was demonstrated and in what context; derive current skill interpretation from that evidence later.

## Cross-app object sharing is intentionally open

A future ecosystem may benefit from shared identifiers or handoffs for concepts such as a person, song, project, era, or asset. That is **not locked today**.

Do not assume:

- Babel World owns every song record
- every sibling app edits the same central project object
- FIRE's project schema should become the universal Babel schema
- a visual-identity product must read or write FIRE internals

Before a second app exists, prepare clean semantic joints where sharing may later happen; do not build speculative runtime infrastructure.

## Fire, local metaphors, and a future personal world

The user's **Fire** is FIRE's strong future progression artifact:

- truthful skill/evidence model underneath
- expressive collectible visualization on top
- begins very small and can become visibly more alive over time
- may become increasingly expressive or character-like within whatever pixel constraints eventually prove useful
- does not claim bigger = objectively better producer
- does not reset because someone took time away

Another future product may have a different local metaphor. A learning product, for example, could theoretically have a **River** or another journey metaphor if that actually fits the product. That example is not a naming decision.

If multiple metaphors someday appear in Babel World, they can coexist or visually interact **without corrupting their meanings**. For example, a River could reflect the light of a Fire as an environmental relationship without mathematically leveling the Fire or pretending unrelated activity proves production mastery.

## Source marks, achievements, and pixel badge language

Keep these semantics separate:

1. **Source/origin marks** — small provenance indicators showing which app(s) contributed credible evidence to a shared skill or record.
2. **Achievement badges** — collectible recognition for meaningful accomplishments, discoveries, milestones, or genuinely cross-app achievements.
3. **App/domain identity marks** — visual identity for an app/domain itself, if useful; these are not automatically achievements or skill evidence.

A future Babel World may provide one centralized place to browse accumulated badges/marks. The presentation remains open.

### Source/origin marks

- show a contributing app once in a useful summary rather than one icon per event
- answer **where evidence came from**, not how skilled the person is
- expand to the underlying evidence/context when useful
- multiple source marks can accumulate without creating a global level

### Achievement badges

Start small and implement extremely well.

A meaningful badge should have inspectable truth behind it: stable identity, meaning/criteria, provenance, and supporting milestone/evidence context when appropriate.

Potential achievements include first finished Recreation/Original, meaningful transfer, recurring diagnosis resolved across projects, deliberate practice, major discoveries, or later cross-app accomplishments.

### Pixel language

A unified original pixel-art language is a strong future hypothesis for Fire, source marks, app/domain marks, and achievement badges.

Desired qualities:

- readable at tiny sizes
- strong silhouettes
- collectible identity
- small expressive state changes
- potentially lightweight sprite animation
- able to scale from tiny provenance marks to a larger dedicated Fire view

Minecraft and classic Pokémon-era pixel art are references for constrained readability, warmth, animation, and collectibility. Do not copy assets, UI, textures, palettes, characters, or sprites.

Exact pixel dimensions, palette, animation frames, growth states, and degree of characterfulness are intentionally open.

## Babel World badge collection

Babel likes the future idea of being able to browse **all earned/meaningful badges in one place**, including app/domain identity marks and specific achievement badges.

Do not assume a complete Fire/Water/Earth/Air collection exists. Only real products and real achievements should appear.

A future collection might include:

- FIRE identity/source marks
- identity marks for whatever sibling products actually earn existence
- production-skill achievements
- cross-app achievements
- hidden/lore discoveries where appropriate
- major project milestones

The collection's visual enclosure remains unresolved and should be discovered later through prototyping.
